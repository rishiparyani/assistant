Privacy policy

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Gigspree is a small, free assistant for your plans, money, reminders, notes and lists, and for chats with the people you plan with. This page explains what it stores, who can see it and how to have it deleted.

What we collect

  • Your account: when you sign in with Google we receive your name, email address and profile photo. With a passkey we keep only its public key. We never see or store a password.
  • What you enter: your lists and what's in them (notes, reminders, events, expenses, and any list you make), the people in them (names, and phone numbers or emails if you add them), your chats with other people, your conversations with the assistant and what you ask it to remember.
  • To run the app: a sign-in cookie (with each signed-in session we also keep the device's IP address and browser type, to keep sessions secure), a history of changes (who changed what and when, so it can be undone), and, if you turn them on, the details needed to send you notifications.
  • Usage counts: how often the assistant is used, how fast it answers, what it costs and how often something fails. These are numbers only, never what you wrote.

We don't use advertising, tracking cookies or third-party analytics.

Who can see it

  • You, and the people you share with. A list you share, or a chat's home, is seen by the people in it, as far as their role allows. Everyone else can't see it at all.
  • Not us. The app team doesn't look at what you keep or write. No screen or tool of ours shows your lists, records, chats or assistant conversations; we see only the usage counts above. To fix problems we use our own test accounts with made-up data.
  • The assistant's AI model. When you ask the assistant something, your message and the parts of your lists it needs are sent to an AI model run by Cloudflare (Workers AI) to work out the answer. For people with faster answers switched on, the same goes to Cerebras (in the US) instead. Neither uses it to train their models.
  • If a list owner shares a link (for example a guest list with a venue), whoever has the link sees only what that link shows.
  • If you connect an AI assistant (such as Claude or ChatGPT) or a Siri Shortcut, it can read and change your lists as you, until you disconnect it in Settings.

We never sell your data or share it with anyone else.

Google user data

Gigspree uses your Google account only to sign you in (name, email and photo). The app team also connects its own Google Drive for nightly backups; this uses the drive.file permission, which only reaches files the app itself creates. Gigspree's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Where it's kept

Data is stored with Cloudflare, which encrypts it on disk, and always travels over HTTPS. It isn't end-to-end encrypted: the app's servers read it to search, run the assistant and carry out your workflows, which is why our own promise above matters. Some secrets, such as the Google Drive connection, are encrypted again by the app. The links and tokens the app gives out (Siri Shortcuts, calendar feeds, share links) are stored only as one-way hashes. The app keeps a copy of your home on your device so it works offline; signing out removes it.

Keeping and deleting

Your data stays until you delete it. An item you delete from a list goes to Recently deleted, where you can bring it back for 30 days; then it's erased for good (or straight away if you choose Delete forever), and the history keeps only who did what and when. Other things you remove (a list, a field, a saved card, a rule, a workflow, a comment, a memory) are hidden at once and erased on request. After erasing, copies can remain for a while only in recovery copies: Cloudflare's, for up to 30 days, and the app's nightly backups, of which the newest 60 are kept. To have your account and data deleted, or anything erased, or to ask what we hold about you, write to privacy@gigspree.in.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. Big changes will be announced in the app.
